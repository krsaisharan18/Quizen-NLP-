The French Revolution began in 1789 and transformed the political landscape of Europe. King Louis XVI summoned the Estates-General in May 1789 to address a severe financial crisis. On 14 July 1789, a crowd of Parisians stormed the Bastille, a royal fortress and prison in Paris. The National Assembly adopted the Declaration of the Rights of Man and of the Citizen in August 1789. This document proclaimed that all men are born free and equal. In 1792, the monarchy was abolished and France became a republic. Louis XVI was executed by guillotine in January 1793. Maximilien Robespierre led the Committee of Public Safety during the Reign of Terror. The Terror claimed about 17,000 official victims. Napoleon Bonaparte seized power in 1799 and later crowned himself emperor in 1804. The Napoleonic Code, introduced in 1804, reformed French law and influenced legal systems across Europe. Napoleon was defeated by British and Prussian forces at the Battle of Waterloo in 1815.
