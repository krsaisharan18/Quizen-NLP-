Photosynthesis is the process by which green plants convert light energy into chemical energy. It takes place mainly in the chloroplasts, which contain the pigment chlorophyll. Chlorophyll absorbs sunlight and uses its energy to split water molecules. The reaction releases oxygen as a by-product. Plants then use carbon dioxide from the air to build glucose, a simple sugar. Jan Ingenhousz discovered in 1779 that plants need light to produce oxygen. The Calvin cycle, named after Melvin Calvin, produces glucose in the stroma of the chloroplast. Cellular respiration is the process in which cells break down glucose to release energy. Mitochondria are the organelles that carry out most of this process. Respiration produces about 36 molecules of ATP from each molecule of glucose. Scientists measure photosynthesis rates by recording the oxygen released by leaves.
